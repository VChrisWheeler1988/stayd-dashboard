Change-load warning, from Stay Texas: Matt ran PMS migration + Breezeway + rebrand in one 90-day sprint and lost Superhost for the first time since 2013. We are planning eight initiatives across eight brands. Sequence per brand, not per initiative.

The fork, 30North vs. Renjoy: 30North is turning maintenance INTO a profit center (30% net margin target). Renjoy deliberately runs it at BREAK-EVEN to avoid driving owners away. Haven loses money on it and is fighting to reach break-even. Two of three sit at or below zero — 30North may be the outlier, not the model.

And the number that may settle it — Haven: 85% of owners are cash-flow negative. Three brands are losing owners because the asset does not pencil. You cannot raise take rate on owners who are already underwater.

Host2Coast closes the argument. The flagship $300K fee increase targets a brand already at a 45% take rate — the wall Stay Texas hit at 48% and retreated from. But the $300K is sitting in his P&L anyway: housekeeping margin at 13% against a 30% target, a $625K amenity program netting at best $200K, and $175K/yr on inspections Haven proved may be unnecessary. Same money, no owner conversation, no occupancy risk. Model the margin path before the fee path.

Discovery complete — all 8 brands. The original plan was: raise fees at three brands, fix the weak ones, set 2027 goals. The evidence says otherwise. Fees are already at the ceiling where we planned to raise them. A large share of owners are underwater — both the churn driver and the reason fees cannot rise. The money is in margin and ancillary: the damage waiver (4-for-4, does not touch owners), housekeeping margin, amenity execution, inspection spend. And every brand already holds a piece of the answer another brand is missing. The highest-value 90-day deliverable may be the exchange itself, not the goals.
